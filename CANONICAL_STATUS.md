# Canonical status of the Epistemic Octahedron

## Current canonical discovery

The current canonical form of the Epistemic Octahedron is Version 1.0, published on July 29, 2026, under DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21688197`.

Version 1.0 is the fixed first disclosure of the discovery. Its geometry, semantic axes, null exception, epistemic regions, referent discipline, plotting conditions, universality claim, and reciprocal challenge standard are not a rolling community-edited framework.

Canonical status does not mean immunity from criticism. It means that preference, popularity, institutional adoption, credentials, terminology, or the existence of a later instrument do not by themselves revise the discovery.

## What remains open

Anyone may:

- study, cite, teach, test, criticize, or attempt to refute the Epistemic Octahedron;
- compare it against competing philosophical or psychological theories;
- identify a contradiction, false premise, missing function, correspondence failure, or other specific defect;
- develop independent downstream instruments, software, visualizers, datasets, interview methods, scoring systems, or validation studies.

Those activities do not automatically alter the canonical EO.

## Reciprocal structural challenge

The paper gives both competing theories and the Epistemic Octahedron a reciprocal burden.

A serious challenge to the EO must identify a specific displacement condition rather than appeal only to abstract fallibility. The Version 1.0 paper identifies the following forms of serious challenge:

1. an internal contradiction;
2. a formed worldview that cannot be located through the five functions;
3. a necessary and irreducible maturity function missing from concern, constraint, information, interpretation, and epistemic stability;
4. a correspondence failure in which the proposed geometry cannot preserve a maturity-relevant distinction.

A competing account may also challenge universality by demonstrating a mature judgment that need not remain governed by one or more of the following: concern for affected persons, materially relevant practical constraint, materially adequate informational grounding, contextual discernment, or reality and correction.

The model remains unwavering when no such defect is demonstrated and corrigible when one is actually demonstrated.

## Canonical correction procedure

A proposed correction remains external to the canonical EO until all of the following occur:

1. the exact claim, definition, equation, or structural relation at issue is identified;
2. the challenge supplies premises, evidence, or a counterexample sufficient to demonstrate the defect;
3. the challenge is evaluated against the paper's own reciprocal structural standard rather than status, preference, or terminology;
4. the result is documented publicly;
5. any accepted substantive correction is issued as a separately numbered version and preserved beside Version 1.0.

Version 1.0 will never be silently overwritten. A future correction would establish a later canonical version while preserving Version 1.0 as the original disclosure and provenance record.

## Derived and competing models

A person may publish a modified, expanded, or competing framework. Such a work should identify itself as independent, derived, modified, or competing. It does not become the canonical Epistemic Octahedron merely by retaining the name, using an octahedron, or claiming improvement.

Repository policy documents may become clearer over time without changing the theory or creating a new paper version. A paper version changes only when the substantive canonical publication changes.
