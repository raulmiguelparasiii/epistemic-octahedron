# Epistemic Octahedron instrumentation policy

## Purpose

The Epistemic Octahedron is the frozen canonical discovery stated in DOI `10.5281/zenodo.22164942`. Instruments are downstream attempts to measure, operationalize, test, visualize, or apply that discovery.

The author does not reserve instrument development to himself. No prior authorization is required to create an independently authored EO-based questionnaire, interview protocol, scoring model, software implementation, visualizer, dataset, research design, or validation study, subject to the paper's rights notice.

Multiple instruments may compete. Their standing should depend on fidelity, transparency, reliability, replicability, predictive usefulness, correction-responsiveness, and empirical evidence rather than institutional ownership or proximity to the author.

## Theory and instrument must remain distinct

An instrument does not revise the EO merely because it introduces a scoring rule, weighting scheme, questionnaire items, thresholds, a data model, uncertainty calculation, visual interface, training procedure, statistical interpretation, or validation result.

Those are operational choices made by the instrument developer. They must be identified as such and remain answerable to the canonical theory.

The canonical paper specifically leaves psychometric scoring, calibration, reliability testing, empirical validation, and the operational procedure for warranting exact coordinate values to downstream instrument work.

## Required disclosure for downstream instruments

An EO-based instrument should disclose, in a form that can be inspected and criticized:

1. the referent or class of referents it assesses;
2. the evidence it accepts and excludes;
3. its items, prompts, observations, or input procedure;
4. its scoring and weighting rules;
5. how evidence is mapped to concern, constraint, information, interpretation, epistemic stability, and the constrained coordinates;
6. how uncertainty and missing evidence are represented, including use of evidence-warranted regions where exact points are not justified;
7. how orientation underdetermination during formation or re-formation is distinguished from evaluator uncertainty;
8. how contextual emphasis is distinguished from structural displacement;
9. what conditions produce positive or negative vertical placement;
10. how referent scope and burden are retained in comparative uses;
11. its assumptions and known limitations;
12. its validation method and results, if any;
13. every departure from or extension of the canonical EO.

A result must not be presented with greater precision or certainty than the evidence and validation justify.

## Attribution and independence

A downstream work should identify the source discovery substantially as follows:

> Based on the Epistemic Octahedron by Raul Miguel Pascual Paras III, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22164942.

Attribution does not imply endorsement, certification, validation, collaboration, or official status.

Independent developers should identify their own names, organizations, release identifiers, licences, and responsibility for all operational choices. They should not present their instrument as authored, approved, validated, or operated by Raul Miguel Pascual Paras III unless that status has been expressly granted and documented.

## Copyright boundary

The canonical paper, LaTeX source, figures, arrangement, and presentation remain subject to `LICENSE-PAPER.txt`.

This instrumentation policy does not grant permission to reproduce, redistribute, translate, adapt, or republish substantial protected expression from the paper or its figures beyond what applicable law permits. Downstream developers may describe and implement the disclosed EO structure in independently authored expression while clearly attributing the discovery and distinguishing their implementation from the canonical publication.

Developers retain responsibility for choosing licences for their own independently authored software, data, instruments, and documentation. Those licences do not alter the rights or canonical status of the paper.

## Open development without canonical drift

Open instrument development means that anyone may attempt a better measurement method. It does not mean that every operational choice becomes part of the EO.

An instrument may improve measurement accuracy, transparency, reliability, or usefulness while the object being measured remains fixed. A modification to the theory itself must be treated as a structural challenge or competing model and evaluated under [`CANONICAL_STATUS.md`](CANONICAL_STATUS.md).
