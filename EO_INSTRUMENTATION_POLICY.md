# Epistemic Octahedron instrumentation policy

## Purpose

The Epistemic Octahedron is the fixed canonical discovery. Instruments are downstream attempts to measure, operationalize, test, visualize, or apply that discovery.

The author does not reserve instrument development to himself. No prior authorization is required to create an independently authored EO-based questionnaire, interview protocol, scoring model, software implementation, visualizer, dataset, research design, or validation study.

Multiple instruments may compete. Their standing should depend on fidelity, transparency, reliability, replicability, predictive usefulness, correction-responsiveness, and empirical evidence rather than institutional ownership or proximity to the author.

## Theory and instrument must remain distinct

An instrument does not revise the EO merely because it introduces:

- a scoring rule;
- a weighting scheme;
- questionnaire items;
- thresholds or classifications;
- a data model;
- an uncertainty calculation;
- a visual interface;
- a training procedure;
- a statistical interpretation;
- a validation result.

Those are operational choices made by the instrument developer. They must be identified as such and remain answerable to the canonical theory.

## Required disclosure for downstream instruments

An EO-based instrument should disclose, in a form that can be inspected and criticized:

1. the referent or class of referents it assesses;
2. the evidence it accepts and excludes;
3. its items, prompts, observations, or input procedure;
4. its scoring and weighting rules;
5. how it maps evidence to the EO functions and coordinates;
6. how uncertainty, missing information, compressed expression, and contextual emphasis are handled;
7. what conditions produce vertical displacement;
8. its assumptions and known limitations;
9. its validation method and results, if any;
10. every departure from or extension of the canonical EO.

A result must not be presented with greater certainty than the evidence and validation justify.

## Attribution and independence

A downstream work should identify the source discovery substantially as follows:

> Based on the Epistemic Octahedron by Raul Miguel Pascual Paras III, Version 1.0, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21688197.

Attribution does not imply endorsement, certification, validation, collaboration, or official status.

Independent developers should identify their own names, organizations, versions, licences, and responsibility for all operational choices. They should not present their instrument as authored, approved, validated, or operated by Raul Miguel Pascual Paras III unless that status has been expressly granted and documented.

## Copyright boundary

The Version 1.0 paper, LaTeX source, figures, arrangement, and presentation remain subject to `LICENSE-PAPER.txt`.

This instrumentation policy does not grant permission to reproduce, redistribute, translate, adapt, or republish substantial protected expression from the paper or its figures beyond what applicable law permits. Downstream developers may describe and implement the disclosed EO structure in independently authored expression, while clearly attributing the discovery and distinguishing their implementation from the canonical publication.

Developers retain responsibility for choosing licences for their own independently authored software, data, instruments, and documentation. Their licence does not alter the rights or canonical status of the Version 1.0 paper.

## Open development without canonical drift

Open instrument development means that anyone may attempt a better measurement method. It does not mean that every operational choice becomes part of the EO.

A later instrument may improve the accuracy, transparency, reliability, or usefulness of measurement while the object being measured remains fixed. A modification to the theory itself must be treated as a challenge or competing model and evaluated under `CANONICAL_STATUS.md`.
